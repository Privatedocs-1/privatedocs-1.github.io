🎤 What Lipwise Does

Lipwise is a small program that lives quietly in the background of your computer and turns your voice into text. You hold down a shortcut, you talk, and when you let go, the words appear in whatever program you are using at that moment. Your email, your document, your chat window, your notes app, a web form — it does not matter. If you can type in it, Lipwise can type into it.

What makes Lipwise different from the dictation tools you may have tried before is where the work happens. The speech recognition runs on your own computer, not on a distant server. That means a fast, dependable response, and it means your voice does not have to leave your machine to become text.

On top of that, an AI editor sits between your voice and your document. It listens for the kinds of things people actually say when they are thinking out loud — "scratch that", "new paragraph", "make that a list" — and it tidies the result into the text you intended, rather than a word-for-word transcript of every hesitation. If you already know how to talk, you already know how to use Lipwise.

✨ What You Can Do With It

Lipwise fits into almost any kind of writing you already do on a computer:

  • Write long emails in a fraction of the time. Speak the message naturally and let Lipwise turn it into a clean, readable email.
  • Correct yourself mid-sentence. Say "scratch that" and the AI editor removes the part you did not mean, exactly the way a person would rephrase out loud.
  • Turn a rambling answer into a list. Ask for a list and the editor formats your spoken points as a tidy bulleted list.
  • Rewrite on the fly. Say "make that shorter" or "say it more politely" and the editor adjusts the text without you touching the keyboard.
  • Keep your hands free. Useful when typing is awkward, slow, or simply not what you want to be doing with your afternoon.
  • Work comfortably with privacy. Because speech recognition runs on your computer, your audio does not need to travel across the internet to be understood.

The same experience works on Windows, macOS and Linux, so you can use the computer you actually prefer.

🗣️ Your First Dictation

Once Lipwise is set up, try this simple exercise to see how it feels:

  1. Open any program where you can type — a text editor, a notes app, or an empty email.
  2. Click into the text area so the cursor is blinking where you want the words to go.
  3. Hold down the shortcut you chose during setup.
  4. Speak at a normal pace, as though you were explaining something to a colleague.
  5. Let go of the shortcut and watch the text appear.

Try a sentence with a correction in it. Say something like: "Let's meet on Tuesday at ten — scratch that, Wednesday at ten." Lipwise will keep the final version and remove the part you took back, which is usually the moment people realise how much time this saves them.

🧠 The AI Editor, Explained Simply

There are two parts to Lipwise, and it helps to think of them separately.

The first part is the listener. This is the speech recognition engine, and it runs on your computer. Its job is to turn sound into words. It is quick, it works without a constant internet connection, and it keeps the audio local.

The second part is the editor. This is the AI that reads the raw words and understands what you actually wanted. It knows that "scratch that" means delete the previous thought. It knows that when you start counting things out loud, you probably want a list. It knows that a spoken email can be tidied into paragraphs. It is the difference between a transcript and a piece of writing.

You remain in control of the editor. You can use it heavily, lightly, or turn its refinements off entirely if you prefer a plain transcript of exactly what you said.

🔒 Privacy by Design

Most voice tools send your audio to a company's servers to be processed. Lipwise takes a different approach: the speech recognition runs on your computer. Your microphone audio is handled locally, so the words you speak stay on your machine as they are turned into text.

For the AI editing step, you choose where the text goes. If you want everything to stay on your computer, you can connect Lipwise to a local AI model. If you prefer a hosted assistant, you can connect Claude or OpenAI instead. Because this is your decision to make, you always know where your words are being handled.

🔌 Works With the AI You Choose

Lipwise is not tied to a single AI provider. During setup, and at any time afterwards, you can select the option that suits you:

  • Local AI — everything stays on your computer, with nothing sent anywhere.
  • Claude — a hosted assistant known for careful, natural-sounding writing.
  • OpenAI — a hosted assistant with broad capabilities for editing and rewriting.

Switching later is a simple change in the settings, so you are never locked in. If you are not sure which to pick, start with whatever option the setup recommends and revisit it once you have used Lipwise for a few days.

💡 Tips for Better Results

  • Speak at your normal pace. Slowing down unnaturally often makes recognition worse, not better.
  • Use a decent microphone. A headset or a laptop microphone in a quiet room gives noticeably cleaner results than a microphone across the room.
  • Say your corrections. Do not stop and reach for the keyboard. "Scratch that" is faster.
  • Dictate in whole thoughts. A full sentence works better than three words at a time.
  • Make your shortcut yours. Pick a key combination you will not press by accident during normal work.
